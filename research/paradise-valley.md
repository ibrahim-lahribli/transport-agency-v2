# paradise-valley — research notes

- Product id: `paradise-valley`
- Researched: 2026-09-30
- Start point: central Agadir, Boulevard du 20 Août area
- Verdict: route and timings in the draft are **broadly sound**. Minor corrections only.

## 1. Verified route

Access: from central Agadir take the **N1 north** along the coast (direction Taghazout/Essaouira) through **Anza → Tamraght → Aourir**. At Aourir turn **left (east)** onto the mountain road toward **Imouzzer des Ida Outanane** (locally the "Honey Route"). The Paradise Valley car park and entrance sit roughly 12 km up that road. The walk to the pools starts from the car park.

- **Total round trip (road):** ~74 km
- **Total driving:** ~1 h 55 to 2 h 30 (normal traffic, no long stops)
- **Stop order check:** the catalogue order (cooperative → pottery → panoramic viewpoint → valley) is geographically sensible: all lie on or just off the N1/Aourir–Imouzzer corridor, and the valley is the natural far end before turning back.
- **Source conflict on distance:** Barceló puts Paradise Valley ~35 km from the centre / ~60 min; tizihike.com gives ~58 km / ~1 h from Agadir (measured to the far end of the valley). Conservative value used: ~35–40 km to the entrance, walk beyond that.

## 3. Practicalities

- **Fixed hours:** none for the argan cooperatives, pottery workshops or the valley itself; visits are informal daytime stops.
- **Entrance fees (research only, checked 2026-09-30):** the argan cooperatives and pottery workshops used by operators on this route generally do not charge a fixed entry; purchases are optional. Local car parking near the valley may carry a small charge. Not published on the website.
- **Seasonality:** water levels in the pools track recent rainfall and vary strongly. Pools can be low or effectively dry in the long dry season (late spring to early autumn). Swimming may be possible after rain and in cooler months; this must always be phrased as "depending on the season". The route is inland and can be very hot in July–August; mornings are cooler.
- **Effort:** mostly vehicle time; the valley walk is on uneven, rocky, sometimes slippery ground. Not suitable for guests with reduced mobility. Footwear with grip is important.
- **Safety norms (typical industry practice, to be confirmed by our operator):** no age or weight limits typical for this excursion; children are usually supervised by parents on the walk. No protective gear required.
- **What to bring:** shoes or sandals with grip, swimsuit and towel, hat, sunscreen, water, cash for lunch and purchases.
- **Etiquette:** at the cooperative and workshop, ask before photographing people; buying something is not obligatory. Modest dress (shoulders/knees covered) is more comfortable at village stops.
